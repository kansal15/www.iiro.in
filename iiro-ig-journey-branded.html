Where to Start Your Internet Governance Journey

Internet governance isn't one organization or one job — it's a mesh of standards bodies, registries, forums, and community processes that keep the internet running. This is a map of the real entry points: fellowships, internships, and communities, organized the way the ecosystem itself is organized.

Before You Start

What "I-Star" Actually Means

The internet has no single government. Instead, a small set of organizations — informally nicknamed the "I-Star" (I*) family — each steward one piece of it: names, numbers, protocols, and policy dialogue. Almost every fellowship you'll find lives inside one of these.

ICANN

Coordinates the domain name system and IP address allocation policy at the global level.

IETF / IAB

Writes the technical standards (RFCs) that let the internet's networks talk to each other.

Internet Society (ISOC)

The membership organization and chapter network that funds, trains, and advocates for an open internet.

IGF

The UN-convened Internet Governance Forum — the world's largest multistakeholder policy conversation, plus national and regional IGFs.

W3C

Standardizes the web layer itself — HTML, CSS, accessibility, and privacy specs.

NRO & the 5 RIRs

The Number Resource Organization coordinates the five Regional Internet Registries that allocate IP addresses region by region.

The Hierarchy

Fellowships, Organized by Who Runs Them

Start at the root — fellowships as a category — then branch into the organization running each one. Every card links to the program's official page so you can check current deadlines yourself; these move throughout the year.

ICANN
Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers

The best-known entry point. Three fellowship rounds a year, tied to ICANN's three annual public meetings, aimed at underserved and underrepresented communities.

Flagship · 3x / year
ICANN Fellowship Program

A funded, mentored track to attend an ICANN public meeting (Community Forum, Annual General Meeting, or Policy Forum), learn the multistakeholder model, and join the alumni network. Applications open roughly four months before each meeting.

icann.org/fellowshipprogram
For students & early professionals
NextGen@ICANN

A shorter, meeting-linked program that introduces students and young professionals to ICANN's work, usually tied to meetings held closer to their region.

icann.org/next-generation
IETF
Internet Engineering Task Force & IAB

The most technical branch of the tree — no formal "fellowship," but the most open door of all: anyone can join a working group mailing list and start reviewing drafts today.

Self-paced · Free
IETF Newcomers & Mentoring

Structured onboarding for first-time attendees of an IETF meeting, including a newcomer orientation and volunteer mentors matched to your area of interest.

ietf.org/newcomers
Research track
IRTF / Applied Networking Research Prize

For those with a research bent — the Internet Research Task Force runs open groups, and ANRP recognizes early-career networking research.

irtf.org
ISOC
Internet Society

The most structured career ladder in the ecosystem — separate tracks for early-career, mid-career, and grassroots advocates, plus the largest chapter network in internet governance.

6 months · Cohort of ~15
Early Career Fellowship

Seminars, mentorship, and an independent internet-strengthening project, with access to scholars from Oxford Internet Institute and American University.

internetsociety.org/fellowships
7 months · Senior professionals
Mid-Career Fellowship

A leadership-track program on digital transformation and internet governance for experienced professionals, culminating in a symposium.

internetsociety.org/fellowships
6 months · Chapter members
Community Advocacy Fellowship

For active ISOC chapter, special interest group, or standing group members who want to build advocacy skills and regional connections.

internetsociety.org/fellowships
IGF
Internet Governance Forum (UN-convened)

The policy conversation itself — global, regional, and national layers, all open to newcomers without a technical background.

Global
IGF Youth Ambassador / Fellowship tracks

Support for young participants to engage with the annual global IGF, often coordinated through the IGF Secretariat and youth coalitions.

intgovforum.org
Regional
APrIGF Youth & Fellowship

The Asia Pacific Regional IGF runs its own fellowship track, often coordinated in partnership with APNIC — a natural next step after a national IGF.

APIGA · APSIG
Regional Academies & Schools on Internet Governance (APAC)

A separate capacity-building track from the IGF itself — short, intensive "schools" that train youth and practitioners in the fundamentals before they walk into a policy meeting. Co-run by ICANN, KISA, APNIC, DotAsia, and ISOC.

5 days · Ages 18–35 · Fully funded
Regional APIGA

The flagship Asia Pacific Internet Governance Academy, held annually in the Republic of Korea. Applications open around March for the July/August cohort; full fellowship covers travel, stay, and meals.

Local APIGA

National-level editions run by local partners (e.g. APIGA Nepal, APIGA Australia) replicating the regional program at home. Top performers are often fast-tracked to the fully-funded Regional APIGA the following year.

APSIG

Asia Pacific School on Internet Governance — a "training of trainers" school, one step up from APIGA, aimed at those who've already attended a national school or IG event. Fellowship covers airfare, shared accommodation, and a cash allowance.

One Level Deeper

The Five Regional Internet Registries

RIRs sit under the NRO and manage IP address and ASN allocation for one region each. If your interest leans technical — routing, addressing, network operations — this is the most direct branch, and most RIR fellowships fund travel to their policy meetings.

Asia Pacific

APNIC Fellowship

Three tracks — Professional, Youth, and Policy — plus a separate APNIC Foundation Research Fellowship and APRICOT conference fellowship. The most actively developed program of the five RIRs, with a five-month project-based format.

Learn More North America

ARIN Fellowship Program

Funds up to 10 fellows per Public Policy and Members Meeting, twice a year, each paired with a mentor from the ARIN Advisory Council.

Learn More Europe, Middle East, Central Asia

RIPE Fellowship Programme

Requires completing the free "RIPE 101" course and a short quiz before applying; covers travel, accommodation, and a full RIPE Meeting ticket.

Learn More Latin America & Caribbean

LACNIC Fellowship

Supports participation in LACNIC's regional meetings and its policy forum (LACNOG), aimed at students and professionals in the region.

Learn More Africa & Indian Ocean

AFRINIC Fellowship & Training

Runs capacity-building and travel support around its member meetings; worth checking current status directly given AFRINIC's ongoing governance transition.

Learn More Umbrella body

NRO / ASO

The Number Resource Organization coordinates all five RIRs and feeds into ICANN's Address Supporting Organization — useful context once you've picked a region.

Closer to Home

Starting from India

You don't need to apply to a global program first. India has its own on-ramp, built specifically to feed people into the I* ecosystem above.

Government-backed · National

NIXI — Internet Governance Internship & Capacity Building Scheme (IGICBS)

Run by the National Internet Exchange of India under MeitY, this internship trains participants in DNS operations, cybersecurity, digital identity, and Universal Acceptance, pairs them with mentors from government and global IG bodies, and pushes top performers toward ICANN, ISOC, APNIC, and IETF engagement as certified "Internet Influencers." It has already run cohorts training 10,000+ participants.

internship.nixi.in
Annual School · India

inSIG — India School on Internet Governance

India's own School on Internet Governance, hosted each year by an ISOC India chapter and run in step with the region's APSIG network. A short, intensive training in IG fundamentals, plus limited fellowships for selected participants.

insig.in
National Forum

India IGF

India's national chapter of the global IGF process — the lowest-pressure place to attend sessions, hear real policy debates, and meet people already working in the space.

indiaigf.in
Youth Network

Youth IGF Movement

A grassroots, youth-led track feeding into national and regional IGFs — frequently the first line on the résumé of people who later win ICANN or ISOC fellowships.

youthigf.com

DiploFoundation

If You Only Do One Thing

A Starting Sequence

If the map above feels like a lot, here's the order most people actually follow.

1
Learn the vocabulary for free

A DiploFoundation short course or the ICANN Learn platform, over a weekend. You need to be able to explain "multistakeholder model" in a sentence before any application will land.

2
Show up locally first

Attend an India IGF or a Youth IGF Movement session, even virtually. This is unpaid, low-stakes, and gives you your first real network.

3
Apply to one national or regional program

NIXI's IGICBS internship or inSIG if you're a student in India, a Local APIGA if one runs in your country, or an APNIC Youth Fellowship if you're technically inclined — all are explicitly designed as feeders into global programs.

4
Then aim global

With one program on your CV, apply to the ICANN Fellowship or an ISOC Early Career Fellowship — both actively look for applicants who show sustained, not one-off, engagement.
